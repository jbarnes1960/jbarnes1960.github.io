Your Path to Closing

Prequal → offer → inspection / VA appraisal → closing

1

Get pre-approved

James reviews credit, income, assets and entitlement (including a VA Certificate of Eligibility, which he can request) and issues a pre-approval you can write with. This kit is an estimate, not a pre-approval.

2

Write the offer

Martha helps you write and negotiate. Share this kit and the Loan Estimate so the offer reflects a realistic VA payment and cash to close, including the tax-exempt scenario if it applies.

3

Open escrow & inspect

Once under contract, the title company opens escrow, you complete inspections, and James orders the VA appraisal and starts underwriting. Reply quickly to document requests.

4

Underwriting & clear to close

Underwriting confirms occupancy, entitlement, income and assets, and the tax-exemption / funding-fee status. You receive a Closing Disclosure at least three business days before closing.

5

Closing

Do a final walk-through, bring cash to close as instructed by the title company ($9,590 in this estimate, subject to change), sign, and get your keys.

Typical timeline
Timing depends on the contract dates, your documents, the VA appraisal, and title turn times. Responding quickly to document requests is the best way to keep the closing date. If a disabled-veteran tax exemption is part of the plan, start that paperwork early with James and the county.

3919 Eagles Nest St · Pre-Approval Checklist

Pre-Approval Document Checklist

Having these ready speeds approval. Your exact list depends on program and situation.

  • Government-issued photo ID and Social Security number
  • Pay stubs for the most recent 30 days
  • W-2s for the last 2 years
  • Federal tax returns for the last 2 years (all pages and schedules)
  • Bank and savings statements for the last 2 months (all pages)
  • Retirement / investment statements if used for down payment or reserves
  • Gift letter and donor bank statement, if any funds are a gift
  • Self-employed / 1099: 2 years of returns, YTD P&L, and business bank statements if requested
  • Landlord contact or 12 months of rent payments, and 2-year address history
  • VA buyers: Certificate of Eligibility (James can request it) and DD-214 or statement of service
  • Documentation of any disabled-veteran tax exemption or VA funding-fee waiver, if applicable
  • Divorce decree or support documents, if applicable
  • Letters of explanation for inquiries, employment gaps or large deposits, if requested
Texas down payment assistance: TSAHC & TDHCA

Texas offers several down-payment and closing-cost programs that can sometimes be used with a VA loan. TSAHC describes assistance of about 2–5% of the loan via grant or deferred second-lien options under programs including Home Sweet Texas and Homes for Texas Heroes (a strong fit for many veterans, first responders and teachers), plus Mortgage Credit Certificates for eligible buyers. TDHCA My First / My Choice Texas Home programs similarly offer 2–5% assistance. Income and purchase-price limits apply; minimum FICO often 620. Sources: tsahc.org/homebuyers/homebuyer-programs and James’s Texas DPA summary (mtg.broker, Nov 1, 2026). This VA estimate already uses 0% down; assistance, if available, could offset cash to close. Ask James whether a TSAHC or TDHCA option could fit. This is not a commitment to lend.
