The Condo Financing Guide

WHAT CHANGED AND WHAT MATTERS MOST
  1. Conventional condo reviews got stricter and simpler at the same time in 2026. Fannie Mae’s Limited Review and Freddie Mac’s Streamlined Review are gone for applications dated on or after Aug 3, 2026. Most attached projects now need a Full Review, but projects with 10 or fewer units (not in a master association) and detached condos skip the project review.
  2. Reserves go from 10% to 15%. For conventional applications dated on or after Jan 4, 2027, the HOA budget must put at least 15% of assessment income into replacement reserves. HOAs that budget 10% to 14% today will fail after that date.
  3. The 50% owner-occupancy test is gone for established projects on conventional investor loans. It still applies to new projects (presales), and FHA still requires 50% owner-occupancy.
  4. Critical repairs and special assessments are the #1 deal-killer. Unfunded repairs over $10,000 per unit, unsafe conditions, or evacuation orders make the building ineligible until the work is done.
  5. FHA and VA need their own approvals. Check the public FHA and VA lists before you list or write an offer. Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac have no public approved list. The lender reviews the project.
1Listing agent checklist

HOA documents

  • ☐ Lender condo questionnaire from the HOA or management company
  • ☐ Recorded declaration/CC&Rs, bylaws, rules and all amendments
  • ☐ State resale certificate or disclosure package
  • ☐ Current approved budget, plus year-end and year-to-date financials
  • ☐ Reserve study (date, funding plan, recommended contribution)
  • ☐ Last 12 months of board minutes

Budget, money and occupancy

  • ☐ Replacement-reserve line is at least 10% of assessment income (15% from Jan 4, 2027)
  • ☐ No more than 15% of units are 60+ days late on dues or on any special assessment
  • ☐ Special assessments: purpose, total, balance remaining, payoff date
  • ☐ Owner-occupied vs. rented unit count
  • ☐ Largest single owner (any person or company owning several units)
  • ☐ Commercial square footage (mixed-use buildings)

Insurance

  • ☐ Master policy declarations page
  • ☐ Per-unit deductible, if any
  • ☐ Does the master policy cover unit interiors (“all-in”) or bare walls?
  • ☐ General liability (at least $1 million per occurrence) and fidelity/crime coverage
  • ☐ Flood zone and master flood policy

Building condition and legal

  • ☐ Structural or mechanical inspection reports from the last 3 years
  • ☐ Repairs needed in the next 12 months
  • ☐ Litigation or pre-litigation (arbitration or mediation)
  • ☐ FHA and VA approval status, with the expiration date
  • ☐ Short-term rental rules, rental pools, front desk, mandatory club memberships
  • ☐ New construction: phase completion, presales, developer control, certificate of occupancy
✓ Green flags
✓ Reserves budgeted at 15% or more, plus a recent reserve study
✓ Under 5% of units delinquent on dues
✓ No special assessments, or only routine ones
✓ Current FHA and VA approvals
✓ Recent clean structural inspection
✓ Master policy with replacement cost and a modest deductible
✓ Mostly owner-occupied, no big single owner
✕ Red flags
✕ HOA named in a lawsuit (especially construction defect)
✕ Special assessment for structural, balcony, roof or garage repairs
✕ Reserves under 10% of the budget
✕ Over 15% of units 60+ days late
✕ Front desk, rental pool, nightly rentals, “resort” in the name
✕ Over 35% commercial space in the building
✕ One investor owns a big block of units
✕ Insurance non-renewed, or a huge wind/hail deductible

2Building types
Detached / “site” condo Easiest
Lenders check: No shared walls, roofs, floors or breezeways. Fannie Mae waives the project review, and Freddie Mac exempts detached units.
Watch for: HOA dues still count in the buyer’s debt-to-income. The HOA master policy (if any) still has to meet insurance rules.
Best path: Conventional is straightforward. FHA may treat a true site condo like a house. Verify FHA/VA treatment with James.
2–4 unit condo (condo-ized duplex or fourplex) Waived review
Lenders check: Fannie Mae waives the project review and Freddie Mac exempts it. Freddie Mac allows at most one commercial unit.
Watch for: Often self-managed with no real budget, no reserves and one shared insurance policy.
Best path: Conventional is best. FHA single-unit approval needs 5+ units, so FHA needs full project approval. Verify
Small project (5–10 units) New in 2026
Lenders check: Since Mar 18, 2026: no project review for Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac if the project is not part of a master association. Insurance rules still apply.
Watch for: If it is inside a master association, it needs a Full Review. Freddie Mac still requires no critical repairs or evacuation orders.
Best path: Conventional is often the fastest path. FHA single-unit approval is limited to 2 FHA loans in projects under 10 units.
Garden / low- and mid-rise (11+ units) Full Review
Lenders check: Full Review: budget and reserves, 15% delinquency test, single-entity limit, litigation, insurance, critical repairs.
Watch for: Thin reserves, old roofs and parking lots, investor-heavy buildings, missing FHA approval.
Best path: Conventional after the lender’s review. FHA or VA if the project is on the approved list (or FHA single-unit approval).
High-rise Extra scrutiny
Lenders check: Everything in a Full Review, plus engineering and inspection reports for elevators, facades, balconies, garages, roofs and life-safety systems.
Watch for: Large special assessments, high master premiums and wind deductibles, dues that push debt-to-income over the limit, post-Surfside inspection findings.
Best path: Read minutes and inspection reports before listing. Ask James to pre-review the building.
High-rise with commercial / mixed-use Commercial ≤ 35%
Lenders check: Commercial space must be 35% or less of the project or the building, above and below grade. Rental apartments and hotels count as commercial. Commercial parking is excluded.
Watch for: Hotel or apartment floors in the same tower. An HOA earning over 10% of its income from a restaurant, spa or club (Fannie Mae). Commercial over 25% in a flood zone may need extra insurance.
Best path: Get the recorded site plan and square footage. FHA allows up to 49% commercial only by HUD exception. Non-QM if over the limit.
Condo-hotel / condotel / rental pool Non-warrantable
Lenders check: Ineligible for Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac and FHA: front desk, rental pools, nightly rentals, use restrictions, hotel management, “hotel” or “resort” in the name.
Watch for: Red flags: units under 400 sq ft, no full kitchen, 75%+ investor or second-home owners, connecting doors, resort amenities.
Best path: Non-QM condotel programs with a bigger down payment and higher rate. Verify current terms with James.
New construction & conversions Presales
Lenders check: Conventional: at least 50% of the project or legal phase sold or under contract to primary or second-home buyers; phase completion. FHA: at least 30% presold, plus a certificate of occupancy.
Watch for: Builder incentives over agency limits, unfinished phases, developer still in control, non-gut conversions of 5+ units (Fannie Mae PERS review).
Best path: Ask the builder which approvals exist (FHA, VA, Fannie Mae PERS or lender review) before you write the contract.
3Agency comparison (key rules)
Fannie Mae
Who approves the project? Lender Full Review in Condo Project Manager (CPM). Fannie Mae PERS for some new conversions.
Owner-occupancy No test for established projects (50% investor limit retired 3/18/2026). New projects: 50% presold to primary or second-home buyers.
Single-entity limit 21+ units: 20%. 11–20 units: 2 units. 5–10 in a master association: 2 units.
Commercial space 35% or less of the project or building.
Reserves At least 10% of assessment income, rising to 15% for applications on or after 1/4/2027. A reserve study can substitute.
Dues delinquency 15% or fewer of units 60+ days late (dues and each special assessment).
Freddie Mac
Who approves the project? Lender review (Established or New project) in Condo Project Advisor. Can rely on Fannie Mae “Approved” status (reciprocal review).
Owner-occupancy Same: retired for established projects (Bulletin 2026-C). New projects: presale test still applies.
Single-entity limit 21+ units: 25%. 11–20 units: 2 units. 5–10 in a master association: 2 units.
Commercial space 35% or less. 2–4 units: no more than 1 commercial unit.
Reserves Same: 10%, rising to 15% on 1/4/2027. Reserve study allowed.
Dues delinquency 15% or fewer of units 60+ days late (dues and each special assessment).
FHA
Who approves the project? HUD project approval (HRAP or lender DELRAP), valid 3 years. Or Single-Unit Approval (spot approval) for one unit.
Owner-occupancy At least 50%. 35–49% allowed for projects 12+ months old via HUD review if 10% or fewer units are in arrears. New construction: 30% presold.
Single-entity limit 10% of units (20+ unit projects). Smaller projects are stricter. Verify
Commercial space 35% or less. Up to 49% by exception.
Reserves At least 10% of the budget.
Dues delinquency 15% or fewer of units 60+ days in arrears.

4Buyer’s agents: common condo deal-killers

Warrantable = the project meets Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac standards. Non-warrantable = it fails a test, so the buyer needs non-QM or portfolio financing (more down, higher rate).

“It’s warrantable, right?”
“Warrantable” means the project meets Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac standards, so a lender can sell the loan. Nobody can promise that until the lender reviews this project for this loan date. A building that passed last year can fail today.
Fix: Send James the project name and HOA contact before you write the offer. Ask the listing agent for the questionnaire if they already have one.
Thin budget and reserves
A reserve line under 10% of the budget (15% for applications from 1/4/2027) makes the project non-warrantable for conventional loans. Many HOAs still budget about 5%.
Fix: Ask for the reserve study. A study can work if the budget includes its highest recommended contribution (no baseline funding). Otherwise plan for FHA/VA (if approved) or non-QM.
Special assessments
The lender must know why each assessment exists. If it pays for a critical repair that isn’t finished, the building is ineligible. The buyer’s share also counts in debt-to-income.
Fix: Get the purpose, amount, balance and payoff date in writing. Negotiate who pays it at closing. If the repair is done, get the engineer’s sign-off.
HOA delinquencies
If more than 15% of units are 60+ days behind on dues or on any special assessment, the conventional and FHA reviews fail.
Fix: Check the questionnaire on day one. A building near 15% can tip over during escrow.
Insurance gaps and price spikes
Master policy without replacement-cost coverage, deductible over 5%, per-unit deductible over $50K, no flood policy in a flood zone, or a non-renewal. Rising premiums also raise dues, which hurts debt-to-income.
Fix: Get the master policy declarations page early. Quote the buyer’s HO-6 (walls-in, plus the per-unit deductible) during the option period.
Investors, rentals and big owners
Conventional established projects no longer have an investor limit, but single-entity limits still apply. FHA still needs 50% owner-occupancy. Short-term rentals can push a building toward “hotel” status.
Fix: Ask the HOA for the owner-occupancy count and the largest owner’s unit count. Read the rental rules.
Litigation
If the HOA is a party to a lawsuit (especially construction defect), the project is usually ineligible unless the case is minor, insured or a dues collection.
Fix: Ask the HOA attorney or manager for a short letter describing the case. James decides if it qualifies as minor.
Expired FHA or VA approval
FHA approvals expire after 3 years, and many HOAs never renew. VA requires its own approval.
Fix: FHA Single-Unit Approval may work (5+ units, 50% owner-occupied, FHA-insured units under 10%). Otherwise, the HOA needs to recertify, which can take weeks. Verify the timeline with James.
Appraisal surprises
Appraisers lean on sales inside the same project and competing buildings. High dues, special assessments and hotel-like features hurt value or trigger extra review.
Fix: Give the appraiser recent in-project sales and the special-assessment facts. Price to in-building comparables.
Timelines and questionnaire fees
Management companies set their own fees and turnaround times for questionnaires, budgets and insurance certificates, and documents often arrive after the option period ends.
Fix: Order everything on day one of the contract. Verify the fee and turnaround with the management company.

Deal-saver timeline

  1. Before the offer: Get the project name. Check the FHA and VA lists. Send James the HOA or management contact. Ask the listing agent for the questionnaire, budget and insurance.
  2. Day 1 under contract: Order the lender questionnaire, budget, financials, master insurance, minutes and the state resale certificate.
  3. Option / due-diligence period: James completes the project review. Flag reserves, assessments, litigation and insurance issues while the buyer can still walk.
  4. Before the appraisal: Confirm the project is warrantable (or approved by FHA or VA). Switch to non-QM early if needed. Don’t wait for the underwriter to find it.
  5. Closing: HO-6 bound (including the per-unit deductible), assessment payoffs settled, HOA transfer fees and dues prorated.

Non-warrantable? Options

  • Non-warrantable condo loans (non-QM): For buildings that fail one or two agency tests (reserves, litigation, single owner, commercial). Usually 10–25% down, priced above agency rates. Verify current terms with James.
  • Condotel programs: For hotel-style projects. Expect a larger down payment (often 25%+), more reserves and a higher rate. Verify
  • DSCR investor loans: For investor buyers, qualifying on the unit’s rent instead of personal income. Condo eligibility varies by investor. Verify
  • Portfolio and bank loans, or cash: Some banks keep non-warrantable condo loans on their books. A cash purchase followed by a later refinance only works if the project becomes warrantable.

Aug 3, 2026Limited and Streamlined reviews retired
Established projects need a Full Review (or the 2–10 unit or detached waiver). Reserve studies must use the highest recommended contribution, and the baseline funding method is out.
Fannie Mae LL-2026-03 · Freddie Mac FAQ
Jul 1, 2026New condo insurance rules
Master policy per-unit deductible is capped at $50,000. An HO-6 is required if the master policy has a per-unit deductible or doesn’t cover interiors. HO-6 deductible max is the greater of 5% or $2,500. Roofs no longer need replacement-cost coverage.

May 26, 2026Faster FHA single-unit case numbers
FHA INFO 2026-10 (May 20): projects with “Expired” or “Rejected – Register SUA” status now get case numbers automatically. Eligibility rules did not change.

Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac made their post-Surfside rules permanent: critical repairs, special-assessment review, inspection reports within 3 years, and the $10K-per-unit unfunded repair limit (Fannie Mae).
