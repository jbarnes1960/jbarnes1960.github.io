The Condo Financing Guide

What listing agents should gather before going live, what kills condo deals for buyers, how Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, FHA, VA and USDA compare, and the big 2026 rule changes.

WHAT CHANGED AND WHAT MATTERS MOST
  1. Conventional condo reviews got stricter and simpler at the same time in 2026. Fannie Mae’s Limited Review and Freddie Mac’s Streamlined Review are gone for applications dated on or after Aug 3, 2026. Most attached projects now need a full review. Detached condos and projects of 2–10 units skip the project review, unless a 5–10 unit project is part of a master association.
  2. Reserves go from 10% to 15%. For conventional applications dated on or after Jan 4, 2027, the HOA budget must put at least 15% of assessment income into replacement reserves. HOAs that budget 10% to 14% today will fail after that date.
  3. The conventional 50% investor-concentration test is retired for established projects (it used to block investment-property loans when fewer than half the units were owner-occupied). New projects still need 50% of units sold or under contract to primary or second-home buyers. FHA still requires 50% owner-occupancy for project approval.
  4. Critical repairs and special assessments are the #1 conventional deal-killer. For Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac, unfunded repairs over $10,000 per unit within 12 months, a failed safety inspection, or an evacuation order make the building ineligible until the work is done.
  5. FHA did not follow the 2026 conventional changes. FHA keeps its own 50% owner-occupancy, 10% reserve and 15% delinquency tests. Check the public FHA and VA lists before you list or write an offer. Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac have no public approved list; the lender reviews the project.
1Listing agent checklist

HOA documents

  • ☐ Lender condo questionnaire from the HOA or management company
  • ☐ Recorded declaration/CC&Rs, bylaws, rules and all amendments
  • ☐ State resale certificate or disclosure package
  • ☐ Current approved budget, plus year-end and year-to-date financials
  • ☐ Reserve study (date, funding plan, recommended contribution)
  • ☐ Last 12 months of board minutes

Budget, money and occupancy

  • ☐ Replacement-reserve line is at least 10% of assessment income (15% from Jan 4, 2027)
  • ☐ No more than 15% of units are 60+ days late on dues or on any special assessment
  • ☐ Special assessments: purpose, total, balance remaining, payoff date
  • ☐ Owner-occupied vs. rented unit count
  • ☐ Largest single owner (any person or company owning several units)
  • ☐ Commercial square footage (mixed-use buildings)

Insurance

  • ☐ Master policy declarations page
  • ☐ Per-unit deductible, if any
  • ☐ Does the master policy cover unit interiors (“all-in”) or bare walls?
  • ☐ General liability (at least $1 million per occurrence) and fidelity/crime coverage
  • ☐ Flood zone and master flood policy

Building condition and legal

  • ☐ Structural or mechanical inspection reports from the last 3 years
  • ☐ Repairs needed in the next 12 months
  • ☐ Litigation or pre-litigation (arbitration or mediation)
  • ☐ FHA and VA approval status, with the expiration date
  • ☐ Short-term rental rules, rental pools, front desk, mandatory club memberships
  • ☐ New construction: phase completion, presales, developer control, certificate of occupancy
✓ Green flags
✓ Reserves budgeted at 15% or more, plus a recent reserve study
✓ Under 5% of units delinquent on dues
✓ No special assessments, or only routine ones
✓ Current FHA and VA approvals
✓ Recent clean structural inspection
✓ Master policy with replacement cost and a modest deductible
✓ Mostly owner-occupied, no big single owner
✕ Red flags
✕ HOA named in a lawsuit (especially construction defect)
✕ Special assessment for structural, balcony, roof or garage repairs
✕ Reserves under 10% of the budget
✕ Over 15% of units 60+ days late
✕ Front desk, rental pool, nightly rentals, “resort” in the name
✕ Over 35% commercial space in the building
✕ One investor owns a big block of units
✕ Insurance non-renewed, or a huge wind/hail deductible

2Building types
Detached / “site” condo Easiest
Lenders check: No shared walls, roofs, floors or breezeways. Fannie Mae waives the project review, and Freddie Mac exempts detached units.
Watch for: HOA dues still count in the buyer’s debt-to-income. The HOA master policy (if any) still has to meet insurance rules.
Best path: Conventional is straightforward. FHA: a true site condo (all detached, owner insures and maintains everything but landscaping) needs no FHA approval. USDA: approval may not be required. VA: project approval may still be needed. Verify
2–4 unit condo (condo-ized duplex or fourplex) Waived review
Lenders check: Fannie Mae waives the project review and Freddie Mac exempts it. Freddie Mac allows at most one commercial unit.
Watch for: Often self-managed with no real budget, no reserves and one shared insurance policy.
Best path: Conventional is best. FHA single-unit approval needs 5+ units, so FHA needs project approval, with 75% owner-occupancy and no units behind on dues.
Small project (5–10 units) New in 2026
Lenders check: Since Mar 18, 2026: no project review for Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac if the project is not part of a master association. Insurance rules still apply.
Watch for: If it is inside a master association, it needs a Full Review. Freddie Mac still requires no critical repairs or evacuation orders.
Best path: Conventional is often the fastest path. FHA single-unit approval allows at most 2 FHA loans in projects under 20 units.
Garden / low- and mid-rise (11+ units) Full Review
Lenders check: Full Review: budget and reserves, 15% delinquency test, single-entity limit, litigation, insurance, critical repairs.
Watch for: Thin reserves, old roofs and parking lots, investor-heavy buildings, missing FHA approval.
Best path: Conventional after the lender’s review. FHA or VA if the project is on the approved list (or FHA single-unit approval).
High-rise Extra scrutiny
Lenders check: Everything in a Full Review, plus engineering and inspection reports for elevators, facades, balconies, garages, roofs and life-safety systems.
Watch for: Large special assessments, high master premiums and wind deductibles, dues that push debt-to-income over the limit, post-Surfside inspection findings. FHA has no matching critical-repair rule, but it still reviews special assessments and HOA finances.
Best path: Read minutes and inspection reports before listing. Ask James to pre-review the building.
High-rise with commercial / mixed-use Commercial ≤ 35%
Lenders check: Commercial space must be 35% or less of the project or the building, above and below grade. Rental apartments and hotels count as commercial. Commercial parking is excluded.
Watch for: Hotel or apartment floors in the same tower. An HOA earning over 10% of its income from a restaurant, spa or club (Fannie Mae). Commercial over 25% in a flood zone may need extra insurance.
Best path: Get the recorded site plan and square footage. FHA allows up to 49% commercial only by exception, and the commercial and residential sides must be financially independent. Non-QM if over the limit.
Condo-hotel / condotel / rental pool Non-warrantable
Lenders check: Ineligible for Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, FHA and USDA: front desk, rental pools (even voluntary ones for Fannie Mae), nightly rentals, use restrictions, hotel management, “hotel” or “resort” in the name. Freddie Mac also rejects HOAs registered for short-term rentals.
Watch for: Red flags: units under 400 sq ft, no full kitchen, 75%+ investor or second-home owners, connecting doors, resort amenities.
Best path: Non-QM condotel programs with a bigger down payment and higher rate. Verify current terms with James.
New construction & conversions Presales
Lenders check: Conventional: at least 50% of the project or legal phase sold or under contract to primary or second-home buyers, and the phase substantially complete. A project stays “new” until 90% of units are sold for Fannie Mae (75% for Freddie Mac). FHA: complete project with a certificate of occupancy and at least 30% owner-occupancy.
Watch for: Builder incentives over agency limits, unfinished phases, developer still in control, non-gut conversions of 5+ units (Fannie Mae PERS review).
Best path: Ask the builder which approvals exist (FHA, VA, Fannie Mae PERS or lender review) before you write the contract.
3Agency comparison (key rules)
Fannie Mae
Who approves the project? Lender Full Review in Condo Project Manager (CPM). Fannie Mae PERS for some conversions. Established projects approved by HUD (HRAP) may be accepted.
Owner-occupancy Established: no test (the 50% investor-concentration limit for investment loans was retired 3/18/2026). New projects: 50% sold or under contract to primary or second-home buyers.
Single-entity limit 21+ units: 20%. 11–20 units: 2 units. 5–10 in a master association: 2 units.
Commercial space 35% or less of the project or of the building it is in.
Reserves At least 10% of assessment income, rising to 15% for applications on or after 1/4/2027. A reserve study can substitute (highest recommended contribution; no baseline funding).
Dues delinquency 15% or fewer of units 60+ days late (dues and each special assessment).
Freddie Mac
Who approves the project? Lender review (Established or New project) in Condo Project Advisor. Can rely on a Fannie Mae or FHA approval (reciprocal review).
Owner-occupancy Same: retired for established projects (Bulletin 2026-C). New projects: 50% presale to primary or second-home buyers.
Single-entity limit 21+ units: 25%. 11–20 units: 2 units. 5–10 in a master association: 2 units.
Commercial space 35% or less. 2–4 units: no more than 1 commercial unit.
Reserves Same: 10%, rising to 15% on 1/4/2027. Reserve study allowed on the same terms.
Dues delinquency 15% or fewer of units 60+ days late (dues and each special assessment).
FHA
Who approves the project? HUD project approval (HRAP or lender DELRAP), valid 3 years. Or Single-Unit Approval for one unit. True site condos need no approval.
Owner-occupancy Project approval: 50% (30% for complete new construction; 35–49% by exception for 12+ month old projects with 10% or fewer units in arrears). Loan in an approved project: 35%. Single-Unit Approval: 50%. 2–4 unit projects: 75%.
Single-entity limit 20+ units: 10%. Under 20 units: 1 unit per owner.
Commercial space 35% or less. Up to 49% by exception. Commercial and residential must be financially independent.
Reserves At least 10% of 12 months of assessments, or an acceptable reserve study (no more than 36 months old).
Dues delinquency 15% or fewer of units 60+ days in arrears (10% under the owner-occupancy exception; none in 2–4 unit projects).
VA
Who approves the project? VA project approval. Projects approved by HUD/FHA or USDA are generally added to the VA list without another review. Verify
Owner-occupancy No published percentage. Verify
Single-entity limit No published test. Verify
Commercial space Mixed-use projects must disclose the nonresidential share. No published cap. Verify
Reserves No published test. Verify
Dues delinquency No published test. Verify
USDA
Who approves the project? No USDA approval. Must be approved or accepted by HUD/FHA, VA, Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac; the lender may certify using that agency’s standards. Verify
Owner-occupancy Follows the approving agency.
Single-entity limit Follows the approving agency.
Commercial space Follows the approving agency.
Reserves Follows the approving agency.
Dues delinquency Follows the approving agency.

4Buyer’s agents: common condo deal-killers

Warrantable = the project meets Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac standards. Non-warrantable = it fails a test, so the buyer needs non-QM or portfolio financing (more down, higher rate).

“It’s warrantable, right?”
“Warrantable” means the project meets Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac standards, so a lender can sell the loan. Nobody can promise that until the lender reviews this project for this loan date. A building that passed last year can fail today.
Fix: Send James the project name and HOA contact before you write the offer. Ask the listing agent for the questionnaire if they already have one.
Thin budget and reserves
A reserve line under 10% of the budget (15% for applications from 1/4/2027) makes the project non-warrantable for conventional loans. FHA also requires 10%.
Fix: Ask for the reserve study. A study can work if the budget includes its highest recommended contribution (no baseline funding). Otherwise plan for FHA/VA (if approved) or non-QM.
Special assessments
The lender must know why each assessment exists. If it pays for a critical repair that isn’t finished, the building is ineligible for conventional loans. The buyer’s share also counts in debt-to-income.
Fix: Get the purpose, amount, balance and payoff date in writing. Negotiate who pays it at closing. If the repair is done, get the engineer’s sign-off.
HOA delinquencies
If more than 15% of units are 60+ days behind on dues or on any special assessment, the conventional and FHA reviews fail.
Fix: Check the questionnaire on day one. A building near 15% can tip over during escrow.
Insurance gaps and price spikes
Master policy without replacement-cost coverage, deductible over 5%, per-unit deductible over $50K, no flood policy in a flood zone, or a non-renewal. Rising premiums also raise dues, which hurts debt-to-income.
Fix: Get the master policy declarations page early. If there is a per-unit deductible, the buyer’s HO-6 must cover at least that amount. Quote it during the option period.
Investors, rentals and big owners
Conventional established projects no longer have an investor-concentration limit, but single-entity limits still apply. FHA still needs 50% owner-occupancy. Freddie Mac rejects HOAs registered for short-term rentals.
Fix: Ask the HOA for the owner-occupancy count and the largest owner’s unit count. Read the rental rules.
Litigation
If the HOA is a party to a lawsuit (especially construction defect), the project is usually ineligible for conventional unless the case is minor, insured or a dues collection. Arbitration and mediation count too.
Fix: Ask the HOA attorney or manager for a short letter describing the case. James decides if it qualifies as minor.
Expired FHA or VA approval
FHA approvals expire after 3 years, and many HOAs never renew. VA requires its own approval, though it generally accepts projects already approved by HUD/FHA or USDA. Verify
Fix: FHA Single-Unit Approval may work: 5+ units, 50% owner-occupied, unit CO at least 1 year old (or occupied), FHA loans capped at 10% of units (2 loans under 20 units), and an automated approval or a manual underwrite at 90% LTV or less. Many lenders don’t process single-unit approvals, so ask early. HOA recertification timelines vary. Verify
Appraisal surprises
Appraisers lean on sales inside the same project and competing buildings. High dues, special assessments and hotel-like features hurt value or trigger extra review.
Fix: Give the appraiser recent in-project sales and the special-assessment facts. Price to in-building comparables.
Timelines and questionnaire fees
Management companies set their own fees and turnaround times for questionnaires, budgets and insurance certificates, and documents often arrive after the option period ends.
Fix: Order everything on day one of the contract. Verify the fee and turnaround with the management company.

Deal-saver timeline

  1. Before the offer: Get the project name. Check the FHA and VA lists. Send James the HOA or management contact. Ask the listing agent for the questionnaire, budget and insurance.
  2. Day 1 under contract: Order the lender questionnaire, budget, financials, master insurance, minutes and the state resale certificate.
  3. Option / due-diligence period: James completes the project review. Flag reserves, assessments, litigation and insurance issues while the buyer can still walk.
  4. Before the appraisal: Confirm the project is warrantable (or approved by FHA or VA). Switch to non-QM early if needed. Don’t wait for the underwriter to find it.
  5. Closing: HO-6 bound (including the per-unit deductible), assessment payoffs settled, HOA transfer fees and dues prorated.

Non-warrantable? Options

  • Conforming non-warrantable programs: Some wholesale lenders now offer conventional programs for non-warrantable condos, for example up to 90% LTV with a 680+ credit score and a full project review. DTI limits and project tolerances vary. Verify
  • Non-QM non-warrantable loans: For buildings that fail one or two agency tests. Commonly about 75–80% LTV (20–25% down), priced above agency rates, with a full project review and larger reserves. Verify
  • Condotel and DSCR programs: Condotels commonly run about 65–75% LTV (25–35% down). Investor buyers can qualify on rent with DSCR loans, but condo rules vary by lender, and some don’t finance non-warrantable condos at all. Verify
  • Portfolio and bank loans, or cash: Some banks keep non-warrantable condo loans on their books. A cash purchase followed by a later refinance only works if the project becomes warrantable.

6Latest guideline changes
Jan 4, 2027Upcoming: reserve minimum rises to 15%
For conventional applications dated on or after Jan 4, 2027, the HOA budget must put at least 15% of assessment income into replacement reserves (up from 10%). FHA stays at 10%.
Fannie Mae LL-2026-03 · Freddie Mac Bulletin 2026-C
Nov 10, 2026FHA Handbook update: procedural only
Handbook 4000.1 Update 18 (issued Aug 12, 2026; mandatory Nov 10) made only procedural condo edits: a new email or self-service-portal step when a Single-Unit Approval case number goes to “Holds Tracking,” and a reference to FHA’s existing free-assumability rule. FHA’s condo standards did not change.
HUD Update 18 · HUD Handbook 4000.1
Nov 4, 2026Fannie Mae allows ADUs in condo projects
A condo unit with one accessory dwelling unit is no longer treated as an ineligible “multi-dwelling unit” (SEL-2026-10).
SEL-2026-10 · B4-2.1-03
Aug 5, 2026Fannie Mae Selling Guide updated
SEL-2026-07 added the March changes to the Selling Guide. The Full Review topic is now B4-2.2-01.
SEL-2026-07 · B4-2.2-01 Full Review
Aug 3, 2026Limited and Streamlined reviews retired
Established projects need a full review (or the 2–10 unit or detached waiver). Reserve studies must carry the highest recommended contribution, and the baseline funding method no longer counts. Fannie Mae’s remaining Florida restrictions ended with Limited Review.
Fannie Mae LL-2026-03 · Freddie Mac FAQ
Jul 1, 2026Per-unit deductible and HO-6 rules
A master policy per-unit deductible can be up to $50,000. When there is one, the buyer must carry an HO-6 at least equal to it, covering the same perils. HO-6 deductible max is the greater of 5% or $2,500.
Fannie Mae B7-3-03 · Freddie Mac Bulletin 2026-C
May 26, 2026Faster FHA single-unit case numbers
FHA INFO 2026-10 (May 20): projects with “Expired” or “Rejected – Register SUA” status now get case numbers automatically. Eligibility rules did not change.
FHA INFO messages
Mar 18, 2026Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac condo overhaul
Waiver and exemption expanded to 2–10 unit projects. The 50% investor-concentration limit was retired for established projects. Florida-specific requirements for new projects were dropped. Master policies may now insure roofs at actual cash value, and the inflation-guard requirement is gone.
Fannie Mae LL-2026-03 · Freddie Mac Bulletin 2026-C
Sep 18, 2023Critical-repair rules made permanent
Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac made their post-Surfside rules permanent: critical repairs, special-assessment review, inspection reports within 3 years, and the $10K-per-unit unfunded repair limit.
Fannie Mae SEL-2023-06
Oct 13, 2021Post-Surfside temporary rules
After the Champlain Towers South collapse, Fannie Mae issued temporary condo requirements on deferred maintenance and special assessments.
