What changed and what matters most

  1. Conventional condo reviews got stricter and simpler at the same time in 2026. Fannie Mae’s Limited Review and Freddie Mac’s Streamlined Review are gone for applications dated on or after Aug 3, 2026. Most attached projects now need a Full Review, but projects with 10 or fewer units (not in a master association) and detached condos skip the project review.
  2. Reserves go from 10% to 15%. For conventional applications dated on or after Jan 4, 2027, the HOA budget must put at least 15% of assessment income into replacement reserves. HOAs that budget 10% to 14% today will fail after that date.
  3. The 50% owner-occupancy test is gone for established projects on conventional investor loans. It still applies to new projects (presales), and FHA still requires 50% owner-occupancy.
  4. Critical repairs and special assessments are the #1 deal-killer. Unfunded repairs over $10,000 per unit, unsafe conditions, or evacuation orders make the building ineligible until the work is done.
  5. FHA and VA need their own approvals. Check the public FHA and VA lists before you list or write an offer. Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac have no public approved list. The lender reviews the project.

2Building types: what changes from one condo to the next

The same buyer can get an easy approval in one building and a flat “no” next door. Here’s why.

Detached / “site” condoEasiest

What lenders check
No shared walls, roofs, floors or breezeways. Fannie Mae waives the project review, and Freddie Mac exempts detached units.
Watch for
HOA dues still count in the buyer’s debt-to-income. The HOA master policy (if any) still has to meet insurance rules.
Best path
Conventional is straightforward. FHA may treat a true site condo like a house. Verify FHA/VA treatment with James.

2–4 unit condo (condo-ized duplex or fourplex)Waived review

What lenders check
Fannie Mae waives the project review and Freddie Mac exempts it. Freddie Mac allows at most one commercial unit.
Watch for
Often self-managed with no real budget, no reserves and one shared insurance policy.
Best path
Conventional is best. FHA single-unit approval needs 5+ units, so FHA needs full project approval. Verify

Small project (5–10 units)New in 2026

What lenders check
Since Mar 18, 2026: no project review for Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac if the project is not part of a master association. Insurance rules still apply.
Watch for
If it is inside a master association, it needs a Full Review. Freddie Mac still requires no critical repairs or evacuation orders.
Best path
Conventional is often the fastest path. FHA single-unit approval is limited to 2 FHA loans in projects under 10 units.

Garden / low- and mid-rise (11+ units)Full Review

What lenders check
Full Review: budget and reserves, 15% delinquency test, single-entity limit, litigation, insurance, critical repairs.
Watch for
Thin reserves, old roofs and parking lots, investor-heavy buildings, missing FHA approval.
Best path
Conventional after the lender’s review. FHA or VA if the project is on the approved list (or FHA single-unit approval).

High-riseExtra scrutiny

What lenders check
Everything in a Full Review, plus engineering and inspection reports for elevators, facades, balconies, garages, roofs and life-safety systems.
Watch for
Large special assessments, high master premiums and wind deductibles, dues that push debt-to-income over the limit, post-Surfside inspection findings.
Best path
Read minutes and inspection reports before listing. Ask James to pre-review the building.

High-rise with commercial / mixed-useCommercial ≤ 35%

What lenders check
Commercial space must be 35% or less of the project or the building, above and below grade. Rental apartments and hotels count as commercial. Commercial parking is excluded.
Watch for
Hotel or apartment floors in the same tower. An HOA earning over 10% of its income from a restaurant, spa or club (Fannie Mae). Commercial over 25% in a flood zone may need extra insurance.
Best path
Get the recorded site plan and square footage. FHA allows up to 49% commercial only by HUD exception. Non-QM if over the limit.

Condo-hotel / condotel / rental poolNon-warrantable

What lenders check
Ineligible for Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac and FHA: front desk, rental pools, nightly rentals, use restrictions, hotel management, “hotel” or “resort” in the name.
Watch for
Red flags: units under 400 sq ft, no full kitchen, 75%+ investor or second-home owners, connecting doors, resort amenities.
Best path
Non-QM condotel programs with a bigger down payment and higher rate. Verify current terms with James.

New construction & conversionsPresales

What lenders check
Conventional: at least 50% of the project or legal phase sold or under contract to primary or second-home buyers; phase completion. FHA: at least 30% presold, plus a certificate of occupancy.
Watch for
Builder incentives over agency limits, unfinished phases, developer still in control, non-gut conversions of 5+ units (Fannie Mae PERS review).
Best path
Ask the builder which approvals exist (FHA, VA, Fannie Mae PERS or lender review) before you write the contract.

4Buyer’s agents: common condo financing problems and how to avoid them

Warrantable

The project meets Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac standards (budget, reserves, delinquency, ownership, insurance, no critical repairs or litigation, not hotel-like). Buyers get standard conventional pricing with as little as 3–5% down for owner-occupants (program limits apply).

Non-warrantable

The project fails one or more tests, so conventional (and often FHA and VA) is off the table. The buyer can still finance, usually with a non-QM or portfolio loan that requires more down and a higher rate. Learn it early, not at the appraisal.

“It’s warrantable, right?”

“Warrantable” means the project meets Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac standards, so a lender can sell the loan. Nobody can promise that until the lender reviews this project for this loan date. A building that passed last year can fail today.

Fix: Send James the project name and HOA contact before you write the offer. Ask the listing agent for the questionnaire if they already have one.

Thin budget and reserves

A reserve line under 10% of the budget (15% for applications from 1/4/2027) makes the project non-warrantable for conventional loans. Many HOAs still budget about 5%.

Fix: Ask for the reserve study. A study can work if the budget includes its highest recommended contribution (no baseline funding). Otherwise plan for FHA/VA (if approved) or non-QM.

Special assessments

The lender must know why each assessment exists. If it pays for a critical repair that isn’t finished, the building is ineligible. The buyer’s share also counts in debt-to-income.

Fix: Get the purpose, amount, balance and payoff date in writing. Negotiate who pays it at closing. If the repair is done, get the engineer’s sign-off.

HOA delinquencies

If more than 15% of units are 60+ days behind on dues or on any special assessment, the conventional and FHA reviews fail.

Fix: Check the questionnaire on day one. A building near 15% can tip over during escrow.

Insurance gaps and price spikes

Master policy without replacement-cost coverage, deductible over 5%, per-unit deductible over $50K, no flood policy in a flood zone, or a non-renewal. Rising premiums also raise dues, which hurts debt-to-income.

Fix: Get the master policy declarations page early. Quote the buyer’s HO-6 (walls-in, plus the per-unit deductible) during the option period.

Investors, rentals and big owners

Conventional established projects no longer have an investor limit, but single-entity limits still apply. FHA still needs 50% owner-occupancy. Short-term rentals can push a building toward “hotel” status.

Fix: Ask the HOA for the owner-occupancy count and the largest owner’s unit count. Read the rental rules.

Litigation

If the HOA is a party to a lawsuit (especially construction defect), the project is usually ineligible unless the case is minor, insured or a dues collection.

Fix: Ask the HOA attorney or manager for a short letter describing the case. James decides if it qualifies as minor.

Expired FHA or VA approval

FHA approvals expire after 3 years, and many HOAs never renew. VA requires its own approval.

Fix: FHA Single-Unit Approval may work (5+ units, 50% owner-occupied, FHA-insured units under 10%). Otherwise, the HOA needs to recertify, which can take weeks. Verify the timeline with James.

Appraisal surprises

Appraisers lean on sales inside the same project and competing buildings. High dues, special assessments and hotel-like features hurt value or trigger extra review.

Fix: Give the appraiser recent in-project sales and the special-assessment facts. Price to in-building comparables.

Timelines and questionnaire fees

Management companies set their own fees and turnaround times for questionnaires, budgets and insurance certificates, and documents often arrive after the option period ends.

Fix: Order everything on day one of the contract. Verify the fee and turnaround with the management company.

Deal-saver timeline

  1. Before the offerGet the project name. Check the FHA and VA lists. Send James the HOA or management contact. Ask the listing agent for the questionnaire, budget and insurance.
  2. Day 1 under contractOrder the lender questionnaire, budget, financials, master insurance, minutes and the state resale certificate.
  3. Option / due-diligence periodJames completes the project review. Flag reserves, assessments, litigation and insurance issues while the buyer can still walk.
  4. Before the appraisalConfirm the project is warrantable (or approved by FHA or VA). Switch to non-QM early if needed. Don’t wait for the underwriter to find it.
  5. ClosingHO-6 bound (including the per-unit deductible), assessment payoffs settled, HOA transfer fees and dues prorated.

When the building is non-warrantable: options

Non-warrantable condo loans (non-QM)For buildings that fail one or two agency tests (reserves, litigation, single owner, commercial). Usually 10–25% down, priced above agency rates. Verify current terms with James.
Condotel programsFor hotel-style projects. Expect a larger down payment (often 25%+), more reserves and a higher rate. Verify
DSCR investor loansFor investor buyers, qualifying on the unit’s rent instead of personal income. Condo eligibility varies by investor. Verify
Portfolio and bank loans, or cashSome banks keep non-warrantable condo loans on their books. A cash purchase followed by a later refinance only works if the project becomes warrantable.

Aug 3, 2026

Limited and Streamlined reviews retired

Established projects need a Full Review (or the 2–10 unit or detached waiver). Reserve studies must use the highest recommended contribution, and the baseline funding method is out.

Fannie Mae LL-2026-03 (is.gd/vcPnCg)Freddie Mac FAQ (is.gd/CBd67I)
Jul 1, 2026

New condo insurance rules

Master policy per-unit deductible is capped at $50,000. An HO-6 is required if the master policy has a per-unit deductible or doesn’t cover interiors. HO-6 deductible max is the greater of 5% or $2,500. Roofs no longer need replacement-cost coverage.

Fannie Mae B7-3-03 (is.gd/M8CVSc)Freddie Mac Bulletin 2026-C (is.gd/gFe04i)
May 26, 2026

Faster FHA single-unit case numbers

FHA INFO 2026-10 (May 20): projects with “Expired” or “Rejected – Register SUA” status now get case numbers automatically. Eligibility rules did not change.

FHA INFO messages (is.gd/bSqBLj)
Mar 18, 2026

Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac condo overhaul

Waiver and exemption expanded to 2–10 unit projects. 50% owner-occupancy / investor limit retired for established projects. Florida-specific restrictions retired.

Fannie Mae LL-2026-03 (is.gd/vcPnCg)Freddie Mac Bulletin 2026-C (is.gd/gFe04i)
Sep 18, 2023

Critical-repair rules made permanent

Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac made their post-Surfside rules permanent: critical repairs, special-assessment review, inspection reports within 3 years, and the $10K-per-unit unfunded repair limit (Fannie Mae).

After the Champlain Towers South collapse, Fannie Mae issued temporary condo requirements on deferred maintenance and special assessments.
