Foreign National & ITIN Home Loans

Key differences at a glance

  1. Residency status decides agency eligibility, not citizenship. Green-card holders qualify for Conventional, FHA, VA and USDA on the same terms as citizens.
  2. Lawful non-permanent residents (work visa or EAD) can still get Conventional. FHA ended eligibility for them for case numbers assigned on or after May 25, 2025, and USDA accepts only “qualified aliens.”
  3. ITIN borrowers: FHA requires an SSN. Fannie Mae accepts an ITIN only when the borrower is also lawfully present. Most ITIN buyers use Non-QM ITIN programs (up to about 80–85% LTV on a primary home for strong files).
  4. Foreign nationals (living abroad) are Non-QM only: second home or investment, usually 25–30%+ down, passport and visa, foreign credit or reference letters, and DSCR, CPA-letter or asset-based income.
  5. Cost rises as you move away from agency loans: agency → Non-QM → ITIN → foreign national typically means higher rates, more down and more reserves.

1Who is who: borrower definitions

Permanent residentHolds a green card (Form I-551). Treated like a U.S. citizen for Conventional, FHA, VA (if a veteran) and USDA.
Non-permanent resident alienLives in the U.S. lawfully on temporary status: work visas (H-1B, L-1, E-2, TN, O-1, etc.) or an Employment Authorization Document (EAD). Eligible for Conventional, but not FHA since 5/25/2025.
DACA recipientHolds deferred action plus an EAD; this is not a green card. Possible on Fannie Mae conventional loans if the lender accepts DACA. Not eligible for FHA or USDA. Many Non-QM ITIN programs accept DACA.
ITIN borrowerFiles U.S. taxes with an IRS Individual Taxpayer Identification Number instead of an SSN. An ITIN is a tax ID, not an immigration status. Usually financed through Non-QM ITIN programs.
Foreign nationalLives outside the U.S. with no U.S. residency, and may visit on a B-1/B-2 visa or the Visa Waiver Program. Not eligible for agency loans. Non-QM foreign national programs finance second homes and investment property only.
Agency vs. Non-QM“Agency” means loans sold to or insured by Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, FHA, VA or USDA. “Non-QM” means private-investor loans with flexible documentation, at higher rates.

B. Checklist by loan type

Conventional · FHA · VA · USDAAgency

Down payment: Conventional 3% primary / 10% second home / 15% investment · FHA 3.5% · VA 0% · USDA 0%

  • Pay stubs covering the most recent 30 days, with year-to-date earnings
  • W-2s for the last 1–2 years (as the AUS or program requires)
  • Self-employed, commission or rental income: personal 1040s and business returns (1120-S, 1065, 1120) with K-1s
  • Bank and asset statements for the last 2 months, all pages, with large deposits explained
  • Signed IRS Form 4506-C (tax transcript request)
  • ID and residency documents for the borrower type (see table above)
  • VA: Certificate of Eligibility. USDA: income for every household member.

DSCR

Down payment: 20% typical (FN 25%+ · ITIN 20%+), investment only

  • No pay stubs, W-2s or tax returns
  • Lease for each rented unit, and/or the appraisal rent schedule (Form 1007 for 1 unit, 1025 for 2–4 units)
  • Short-term rental: 12 months of rental income history (rules vary)
  • Asset statements (2 months) for the down payment, closing costs and reserves
  • LLC documents if buying in an entity: articles, operating agreement, EIN
  • Landlord insurance quote; ID per borrower type

Bank Statement

Down payment: 10–15% primary · about 20% second home / investment

  • 12 or 24 months of personal or business bank statements, all pages
  • CPA, EA or tax-preparer letter confirming ownership and self-employment history (usually 2 years)
  • Business statements: an expense factor, an expense letter, or a third-party P&L
  • Proof the business is active (license or state registration)
  • Asset statements for the down payment and reserves; no tax returns

P&L Only

Down payment: 15–20% primary / second home · 20%+ investment

  • 12 or 24-month P&L prepared or signed by a CPA, EA or licensed tax preparer
  • Proof of self-employment history
  • Some programs: 2 months of business bank statements to support the P&L
  • Asset statements for the down payment and reserves; no tax returns

Asset Depletion / Utilization

Down payment: 20–25% (75–80% LTV)

  • Statements for every qualifying account (checking, savings, brokerage, retirement)
  • Assets must be seasoned (often 2–3 months or more)
  • Retirement account terms (to confirm access)
  • No employment or income documents needed

ITIN programsNon-QM

Down payment: 15–20% primary · about 25–30% second home · 20–30% investment

  • ITIN assignment letter (IRS CP565) or ITIN card. Alt-doc programs may accept a recent IRS letter, an executed W-7, or a tax-preparer letter.
  • ITIN tax returns (1040s) and IRS transcripts for full doc; 2 years of ITIN filings is common
  • Income docs for the chosen method (pay stubs/W-2s, 12–24 months of bank statements, P&L, 1099s or WVOE). All must show the borrower’s own ITIN.
  • Unexpired government photo ID
  • U.S. credit report under the ITIN, and 12-month rent or mortgage history on some programs
  • Proof of the borrower’s own funds (10% minimum is common)

Foreign National programsNon-QM

Down payment: 25%+ (second home / investment only); 30–45% on larger loans

  • Passport + visa (or I-797 + I-94); proof of foreign home address
  • International credit report or credit reference letters
  • Foreign bank statements translated to English and converted to U.S. dollars (xe.com or Wall Street Journal rate)
  • Down payment and closing funds wired to a U.S. account before closing (some programs require an ACH form)
  • Income: CPA letter (2 years + YTD) for full doc, or a lease / 1007 for DSCR
  • Documents signed abroad are often notarized at a U.S. embassy or consulate

Loan typeGift funds allowed?Borrower’s own-funds minimum
ConventionalYes on primary and second homes; not on investment propertyNone on 1-unit primary homes (or any primary or second home at 80% LTV or less). 5% own funds on 2–4 unit primary and second homes over 80% LTV.
FHAYes, from acceptable donors such as familyNone beyond the 3.5% minimum, which may itself come from a gift
VA / USDAYes (no down payment is required)None
DSCR / Bank Stmt / P&L / AssetOften, by program; gifts usually can’t count toward reservesVaries by program
ITIN programsOften, by program10% own funds is common
Foreign NationalSome programs10% own funds is common; gifts can’t be used for reserves
  • Gift letter (signed by the donor): the dollar amount, a statement that no repayment is expected, and the donor’s name, address, phone and relationship. Include proof of transfer, such as the donor’s check and the borrower’s deposit slip, or a wire to the borrower or title company.
  • Seasoning: lenders usually review the last 2 months of statements, and any large deposit that isn’t payroll must be sourced. Foreign national programs typically require funds sourced and seasoned for about 30–60 days, then moved to a U.S. account before closing.
  • Translations: any document not in English (bank statements, CPA letters, reference letters) must be translated, with foreign balances converted to U.S. dollars.

6Which loan fits? Quick scenarios

Canadian buyer, vacation condo, no U.S. credit→ FN second home (CPA-letter full doc or asset-based). Plan on about 25–30% down plus reserves.
Investor from Mexico buying a rental→ Foreign national DSCR. The rent covers the payment, so no U.S. income is needed. About 25–30%+ down.
Self-employed contractor who files taxes with an ITIN→ ITIN bank statement or P&L, primary residence, as little as about 15–20% down with a strong score.
ITIN investor buying a duplex→ ITIN DSCR. About 20% down with a 700+ score, more with no score.
H-1B engineer with an SSN, first home→ Conventional (as little as 3–5% down). FHA is no longer an option for visa holders.
Green-card holder with a 600 credit score→ FHA at 3.5% down (580+), same as a citizen.
DACA recipient with SSN and EAD→ A Fannie Mae conventional loan through a lender that accepts DACA, or an ITIN/Non-QM program. Not FHA or USDA.
Retiree abroad with large U.S. brokerage account→ FN asset-utilization (select programs) or DSCR if it’s a rental.
Verify before you write an offer: lender overlays often add to the agency rules (for example DACA on conventional loans, VA residency documentation, and minimum scores). Non-QM matrices change often, and some programs are limited by state (Texas cash-out, OFAC-sanctioned countries, certain counties). I’ll confirm the current guidelines for your exact scenario.
