Manufactured & Modular Home Financing Guide

Mobile, manufactured or modular? How to tell them apart, why it changes the loan, a resale checklist, and how FHA, VA, USDA, Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac compare, including the 2025–2026 rule changes.

Last updated November 16, 2026 · Reviewed quarterly (next review February 2027)

WHAT MATTERS MOST
  1. “Mobile,” “manufactured” and “modular” are three different things to a lender. A mobile home was built before June 15, 1976 and can’t get an agency mortgage. A manufactured home is built to the federal HUD code on a permanent chassis. A modular home is built to the state or local building code and is financed like a site-built house.
  2. The red HUD tags and the HUD data plate prove it’s a HUD-code home. Every section gets a metal HUD Certification Label outside, and there’s a paper data plate inside. If they’re missing, the lender can order a label verification letter from IBTS.
  3. Agency mortgages need real property. FHA Title II, VA, USDA, Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac all require the home to be permanently affixed and legally treated as real estate with the land. A home still titled like a vehicle, or sitting on a rented lot in a park, usually needs a chattel loan or FHA Title I instead.
  4. The foundation and the move history make or break the deal. FHA wants an engineer’s certification that the foundation meets HUD’s Permanent Foundations Guide. FHA, Fannie Mae and USDA reject homes moved from another site. Since Sept 2, 2026, Freddie Mac accepts a moved home after an engineer’s inspection.
  5. 2025–2026 opened doors. USDA now guarantees loans on existing manufactured homes up to 20 years old (implemented May 2025). Freddie Mac cut its minimum size to 400 sq ft (Feb 2026). Fannie Mae allows 2–4 unit and multi-story manufactured homes under UAD 3.6. Freddie Mac accepts moved homes (Sept 2026). The MH Advantage and CHOICEHome standards were aligned (June 4, 2026).
1Mobile vs. manufactured vs. modular
Mobile home (pre-HUD code) Not eligible
Built to: No federal code. Built before June 15, 1976, when the HUD code took effect.
How to spot it: No HUD certification label. Often a single-wide on a steel frame; the year on the title or serial number is before 1976.
Financing: Not eligible for FHA, VA, USDA, Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac mortgages. Cash, a personal or chattel loan, or a land-value-only deal.
Manufactured home (HUD code) HUD code
Built to: Federal Manufactured Home Construction and Safety Standards (24 CFR 3280). Built on a permanent steel chassis and towed to the site in one or more sections.
How to spot it: Red/aluminum HUD Certification Label (2×4 in.) outside on each section, plus a paper HUD Data Plate inside (often in a kitchen cabinet, a bedroom closet or near the electrical panel).
Financing: Agency-eligible only when it’s real property on a permanent foundation and meets each agency’s rules (age, size, foundation, never moved). Otherwise chattel or FHA Title I.
Enhanced manufactured: MH Advantage® / CHOICEHome® / CrossMod™ Best MH pricing
Built to: Still HUD code, built to the GSEs’ aligned specs: roof pitch, eaves, energy efficiency, finishes, masonry or poured perimeter foundation, covered porch.
How to spot it: An MH Advantage sticker or CHOICEHome label next to the HUD data plate. “CrossMod” is an industry name, not an agency program, so it only gets these benefits if it carries one of those labels.
Financing: Fannie Mae: up to 97% LTV (1 unit), standard mortgage insurance, lower price adjustments. Appraisers compare it to other MH Advantage or CHOICEHome sales, or site-built homes when needed.
Modular home (state/local code) Like site-built
Built to: Built in a factory to the state or local building code (typically the International Residential Code), then set on a site-built foundation by a contractor.
How to spot it: No HUD label. Usually a state modular label or certificate (often inside a cabinet or the electrical panel). Typically no permanent chassis, though some “on-frame” modulars have one.
Financing: Financed like a site-built house by Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, FHA and VA. No minimum width; comparable sales can include site-built homes.

Which home is it? Quick identifier

  1. HUD label or HUD data plate? Yes = HUD-code manufactured home (step 2). No = step 3.
  2. MH Advantage sticker or CHOICEHome label? Yes = enhanced manufactured home (best conventional terms). No = standard manufactured home.
  3. State modular label, or built to state or local code? Yes = modular, financed like site-built. No = step 4.
  4. Built before June 15, 1976? Yes = pre-HUD mobile home, not eligible for agency loans. Not sure = order an IBTS label verification letter.

2How the differences affect financing
Real property vs. personal property
Every agency mortgage must be secured by the home and the land as one real estate parcel. The vehicle title must be purged, surrendered or cancelled per state law (or the home must be exempt from titling), often with a recorded affidavit of affixation. Taxes differ: USDA requires the home to be taxed as real estate. FHA says it doesn’t have to be. Fannie Mae requires legal real-property classification only. Freddie Mac says real-estate taxation isn’t proof of conversion and allows personal-property taxation where state law requires it. Some lenders require real-estate taxation on every program.
Permanent foundation
FHA requires a foundation that meets HUD’s Permanent Foundations Guide for Manufactured Housing (PFGMH), certified by a licensed engineer or architect. A prior FHA certification can be reused if nothing has changed. Standard Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac loans need a permanent foundation built to the manufacturer’s instructions and HUD and local codes, but no PFGMH engineer letter. MH Advantage and CHOICEHome need an engineered, certified PFGMH foundation with a masonry or poured concrete perimeter. Many lenders want an engineer’s certification on VA and USDA loans too.
Age and the HUD code
All five agencies require HUD-code homes, which means built on or after June 15, 1976. USDA adds age caps: a new home must be manufactured within 12 months of closing, and an existing home within 20 years of closing. Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, FHA and VA publish no maximum age, but condition and comparable sales still matter.
Moved homes
FHA, Fannie Mae and USDA require that the home went straight from the factory or dealer to this site and was never installed anywhere else. Since Sept 2, 2026, Freddie Mac accepts a moved home if a licensed engineer (or the state) inspects it and the new site isn’t in a tougher wind, roof-load or thermal zone; the loan can’t pay moving or setup costs. VA’s text is silent and lenders differ Verify. Many lenders still refuse moved homes on any program.
Land: owned vs. leased
Most agency loans need the buyer to own the land. Exceptions: FHA allows a leasehold (99-year renewable lease, or a lease running 10+ years past the loan). Fannie Mae allows leased land only in a PERS-approved condo or PUD. Freddie Mac allows multi-wide homes in ground-lease condo or PUD communities. USDA needs a leasehold with 45+ years left (shorter in nonprofit land-lease communities). In a typical rented-lot park, the home is personal property: chattel or FHA Title I.
Size and width
FHA and USDA: at least 400 sq ft. VA: 400 sq ft for a single-wide, 700 sq ft for a double-wide. Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac: at least 12 ft wide and 400 sq ft above grade. Freddie Mac lowered its minimum from 600 sq ft in Feb 2026. Single-wides face extra limits on conventional loans (primary residence only, no cash-out), and some lenders require a double-wide on every program.
Appraisal and comparable sales
Manufactured homes are appraised on Form 1004C, or on the redesigned URAR under UAD 3.6 (required for new conventional appraisals since Nov 2, 2026). Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac and FHA want at least two manufactured-home comparable sales, and a single-wide needs a single-wide comparable when available. A site-built home can be the third comparable sale. Appraisers can’t build a comparable by adding a vacant lot sale to a home’s price.
Modular = site-built
Modular homes are not “manufactured housing” to any agency. Fannie Mae and FHA tell appraisers to treat them like stick-built homes, and VA lets comparables include modular, site-built or manufactured homes. If a modular was delivered with transport hardware or has a vehicle title, the lender still needs the hardware removed and the title converted to real property.
3Resale checklist

Ownership, title and land

  • ☐ Real property or personal property?
  • ☐ Title retired, surrendered or affidavit of affixation recorded
  • ☐ Land owned vs. leased (park or community)
  • ☐ Lot rent and land-lease terms (if leased)
  • ☐ Property tax status

The home: tags, age and history

  • ☐ HUD Data Plate inside the home
  • ☐ Red HUD Certification Label on every section
  • ☐ Missing tags? Order an IBTS label verification letter
  • ☐ Built on or after June 15, 1976
  • ☐ Single-wide or multi-wide, width and square footage
  • ☐ Ever moved from another site?
  • ☐ Additions, alterations or structural changes
  • ☐ MH Advantage sticker or CHOICEHome label

Foundation and site

  • ☐ Foundation type
  • ☐ Engineer’s foundation certification (HUD PFGMH)
  • ☐ Wheels, axles and towing hitch removed
  • ☐ Skirting or perimeter enclosure
  • ☐ Utilities permanently connected
  • ☐ Flood zone and elevation

Market, condition and buyer fit

  • ☐ Comparable manufactured-home sales
  • ☐ Condition: roof, floors, plumbing, marriage line
  • ☐ Zoning and HOA or park rules
  • ☐ Chattel or Title I backup for the buyer
✓ Green flags
✓ Home and land titled together as real estate; title retired
✓ HUD data plate and every HUD label present
✓ Built within the last 20 years (inside USDA’s age limit too)
✓ Engineer’s PFGMH foundation certification on file
✓ Multi-wide on owned land, never moved
✓ MH Advantage or CHOICEHome label
✕ Red flags
✕ Built before June 15, 1976 (pre-HUD “mobile home”)
✕ Vehicle title still active, or home on a rented lot in a park
✕ Moved from another site after its first installation
✕ Wheels, axles or tongue still attached; no skirting
✕ Unpermitted additions or structural changes
✕ In a flood zone with no elevation certificate

Common resale problems

“It’s a modular” (but it isn’t)
Sellers and listings often call a HUD-code home “modular.” The appraiser will find the HUD label, and the loan, comparable sales and program change mid-escrow.
Fix: Find the HUD label and data plate (or the state modular label) before you list. Describe the home accurately in the MLS.
Title never retired
The vehicle title is still active, or the affidavit of affixation was never recorded, so the home is still personal property.
Fix: Order a title search plus the state DMV or titling-agency record at listing. Budget 2–6 weeks to retire the title, depending on the state Verify.
No foundation certification
FHA (and many lenders for VA and USDA) need an engineer’s PFGMH certification. Piers and tie-downs alone may not pass, and repairs take time.
Fix: Order the engineer’s inspection before you list. If upgrades are needed, price them in or complete them first.
Missing HUD tags or data plate
Labels get painted over, removed with siding work or lost in remodels. USDA requires both; Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac need one of them (or an alternative).
Fix: Order an IBTS label verification letter or duplicate data plate early; it isn’t instant.
Additions and porches
Room additions, roofs over the home and enclosed porches need a state inspection or a structural engineer’s report. USDA allows only engineered or inspected porches and decks on existing homes.
Fix: Collect permits and finals. If there’s no paperwork, get the structural engineer’s letter before going live.
Thin comparables
Too few recent manufactured-home sales (or single-wide sales) in the area can sink the appraisal or force a low value.
Fix: Give the appraiser recent manufactured-home sales, including older sales or ones from competing neighborhoods. Price with manufactured-home comparables, not site-built ones.
4FHA / VA / USDA vs. Fannie Mae / Freddie Mac
FHA (Title II)
Construction and age: HUD code, built on or after 6/15/1976 (HUD label, or an IBTS label verification letter).
Foundation: Permanent foundation per HUD PFGMH, certified by a licensed engineer or architect on every loan. A prior FHA certification can be reused if nothing has changed.
Title and real property: Home and land must be real estate at closing; any chattel title purged. Doesn’t have to be taxed as real estate.
Minimum size and width: 400 sq ft. No width rule.
Max LTV / down payment: Standard FHA: 96.5% (580+ score), 90% (500–579).
MI / fees: Upfront MIP 1.75%; standard annual MIP. No MH add-on.
Single-wide: Eligible.
Leased land: Allowed: renewable 99-year lease, or a lease running 10+ years past maturity.
Moved homes: Not eligible: must come directly from the factory or dealer.
Cash-out refinance: 80% LTV; the home must have been permanently installed more than 12 months before case number assignment.
VA
Construction and age: Must meet HUD construction standards, so effectively 6/15/1976 or later Verify.
Foundation: Permanent foundation meeting state and local requirements Verify. Lenders typically require a PFGMH engineer certification.
Title and real property: Home and site must be real estate under state law Verify.
Minimum size and width: 400 sq ft single-wide; 700 sq ft double-wide Verify.
Max LTV / down payment: Up to 100% (subject to entitlement) Verify.
MI / fees: Standard VA funding fee Verify.
Single-wide: Eligible at 400 sq ft Verify.
Leased land: Not addressed in VA’s manufactured-home text Verify.
Moved homes: Not addressed; lenders differ Verify.
Cash-out refinance: Cash-out with lot is covered in VA’s manufactured-home tables Verify.
USDA Guaranteed
Construction and age: HUD code. New units: manufactured within 12 months of closing. Existing units: manufactured within 20 years of closing.
Foundation: New units: PFGMH and HUD foundation rules. Existing units: permanent foundation with certification.
Title and real property: Real-estate lien; title surrendered where state law allows (else lien noted on it). Must be taxed as real estate.
Minimum size and width: 400 sq ft.
Max LTV / down payment: Up to 100% of appraised value, plus the financed guarantee fee.
MI / fees: 1% upfront guarantee fee; annual fee 0.35% Verify.
Single-wide: Eligible.
Leased land: Leasehold needs 45+ years unexpired; new homes in nonprofit land-lease communities or on Tribal land: lease 2+ years past the loan term.
Moved homes: Not eligible.
Cash-out refinance: No cash-out Verify.
Fannie Mae (std / MH Advantage)
Construction and age: HUD code, built on or after 6/15/1976. No maximum age. 2–4 unit or multi-story MH (UAD 3.6): built on or after Sept 16, 2024.
Foundation: Standard MH: permanent foundation per the manufacturer, soil conditions and local codes. No PFGMH engineer letter required. MH Advantage: engineered, certified PFGMH foundation with a masonry or poured perimeter.
Title and real property: Home and land legally classified as real property. Title surrendered (or lien noted where the state doesn’t allow surrender). Affidavit of Affixture; ALTA 7, 7.1 or 7.2.
Minimum size and width: 12 ft wide and 400 sq ft above grade.
Max LTV / down payment: Standard MH: 95% purchase or limited cash-out (the matrix’s 97% is MH Advantage only). MH Advantage: 97% (fixed rate, 1 unit). Second home 90%. 2–4 units 90% (MH Advantage 95%).
MI / fees: Standard MH: MI coverage 12/25/30% at 85/90/95% LTV, plus a 0.50% MH price adjustment Verify. MH Advantage: site-built MI coverage, no MH price adjustment.
Single-wide: Primary residence, purchase or limited cash-out only. MH Advantage single-wides eligible (Sep 2025).
Leased land: Only in a PERS-approved condo or PUD on leased land (borrower owns the home).
Moved homes: Not eligible (never installed or occupied elsewhere).
Cash-out refinance: Multi-width only, 65% LTV; 12 months of ownership. No cash-out on single-widths.
Freddie Mac (MH / CHOICEHome)
Construction and age: Built on or after 6/15/1976 to the HUD code. No maximum age.
Foundation: Permanent foundation per HUD codes (installs before 10/20/2008: manufacturer or engineer design). No PFGMH letter for standard MH. CHOICEHome: engineered PFGMH foundation with a masonry perimeter.
Title and real property: Real property with title-surrender procedures and a recorded affidavit; ALTA 7 or 7.1. Real-estate taxation isn’t proof of conversion; may be taxed as personal property if state law requires.
Minimum size and width: 12 ft wide and 400 sq ft above grade (was 600 sq ft until Feb 2026).
Max LTV / down payment: Standard MH: 95% (LPA Accept). Caution: 90%, or up to 95% on a 20-year term. Second home 85%. CHOICEHome 95%; 97% with Home Possible / HomeOne / HeritageOne.
MI / fees: Standard MI coverage (Guide 4701.1). 0.500% MH credit fee at all LTVs (from Oct 1, 2026); CHOICEHome exempt.
Single-wide: Primary residence; purchase or no cash-out only; LPA Accept required. In a condo only with reciprocal project review.
Leased land: Multi-wide only, in a ground-lease condo or PUD community (Ch. 5706): purchase or no cash-out, 95% max.
Moved homes: Eligible since Sept 2, 2026 with an engineer or authority inspection, if not moved into a tougher wind, roof-load or thermal zone. Loan can’t pay moving or setup costs.
Cash-out refinance: Multi-wide only, 65% LTV; 12 months of ownership (6 months if owned free and clear).

The full side-by-side table (MI, appraisal, occupancy and more) is in the online guide. Lender overlays may apply.

When the home stays personal property

FHA Title I manufactured home loans
Insured loans for a home that can stay personal property, including on a leased lot (lease of at least 3 years). Limits in HUD’s handbook (case numbers on or after Mar 29, 2024; indexed annually, current-year figures Verify): home only $105,532 single-section / $193,719 multi-section; lot only $43,377; home + lot $148,909 / $237,096. Up to 95% LTV (90% for scores of 500 or below); 2.25% upfront and 1.00% annual insurance premium. Max terms: 20 years + 32 days (home only), 15 years + 32 days (lot), 25 years + 32 days (multi-section home + lot). Few lenders offer it Verify.
Chattel (personal-property) loans
Many lenders finance a manufactured home as personal property: a rented lot, a home that keeps its vehicle title, or a home that fails agency foundation rules. These are not FHA Title II, VA, USDA, Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac mortgages. Expect shorter terms, higher rates and larger down payments than real-estate loans Verify.
VA manufactured-home loans (not affixed)
VA’s handbook directs lenders on homes that are not permanently affixed to call VA for instructions; the funding fee on these is 1.00% Verify. Lenders rarely offer them.
Lender overlays may apply
Lenders can be stricter than the agencies, and many are with manufactured homes. Rules some lenders add: double-wide minimum, real-estate taxation on every program, no moved homes even on Freddie Mac loans, no leased land or manufactured-home condos, an engineer’s foundation certification on every loan, 12 months of seasoning on any refinance, shorter cash-out terms, higher credit-score minimums, or no conventional ARMs. Some exclude certain states. James checks which lender fits the home before you list or write the offer.
5Recent guideline changes
Nov 10, 2026FHA Handbook 4000.1 Update 18: manufactured housing definition refined
FHA made its definition of Manufactured Housing consistent across Handbook 4000.1 (no new eligibility tests) and refined flood-zone property rules. The manufactured-home cash-out rule (home permanently installed more than 12 months before case number assignment) is in II.A.8.d.v. Issued Aug 12, 2026.
HUD Update 18 · Handbook 4000.1
Nov 2, 2026UAD 3.6 required for new appraisals; URAR replaces Form 1004C
Fannie Mae requires UAD 3.6 for new appraisals submitted on or after Nov 2, 2026 (unless the lender has a temporary exception). Under UAD 3.6, manufactured homes are appraised on the redesigned URAR instead of Form 1004C, with new comparable-sale rules for single-section, multi-story and 2–4 unit homes.
UAD 3.6 policy supplement
Oct 7, 2026Freddie Mac: single-wide homes in condo projects
A single-wide home in a condo project is eligible if the project passes Freddie Mac’s reciprocal project review (Guide 5703.3).
Guide chapter 5703
Oct 1, 2026Freddie Mac credit fees standardized; MH fee stays 0.500%
Freddie Mac’s credit fee grids no longer vary by credit score model. The manufactured-home credit fee stays at 0.500% at every LTV, and CHOICEHome is exempt (settlement dates on or after Oct 1, 2026).
Freddie Mac Exhibit 19
Sep 2, 2026Freddie Mac accepts manufactured homes moved from another site
Effective immediately: a licensed engineer or government authority must inspect the home for structural integrity, and the new site can’t be in a tougher wind, roof-load or thermal zone. Loan proceeds can’t pay delivery, setup, foundation, site or utility costs. FHA, Fannie Mae and USDA still prohibit moved homes, and many lenders do too.
Freddie Mac Bulletin 2026-12
Jun 12, 2026HUD proposes to redefine “manufactured home” for multi-story homes
HUD proposed letting a transportable section that serves as part of an upper floor skip the permanent chassis requirement. This is a proposed rule (comments closed Aug 11, 2026). Until it is final, every lending definition still requires a permanent chassis.
Federal Register 2026-11851
Jun 4, 2026MH Advantage and CHOICEHome standards aligned
Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac now use matching specs for foundation, roof pitch, energy efficiency, exterior features and site improvements, for applications dated on or after June 4, 2026. New single-section homes built after that date need an attached garage or carport (infill-lot exception). Driveway, sidewalk, gutters and a 72 sq ft covered porch are required.
Fannie Mae SEL-2026-01 · Freddie Mac Bulletin 2026-1 · MH Advantage specs · CHOICEHome specs
Mar 31, 2026Fannie Mae: 2–4 unit and multi-story MH, plus ADUs (UAD 3.6)
For lenders using UAD 3.6, loans sold on or after Mar 31, 2026 can be secured by 2–4 unit or multi-story manufactured homes built on or after Sept 16, 2024, a standard manufactured home with one ADU, or an MH Advantage home with ADUs (four units total). 2–4 unit MH is capped at 90% LTV (MH Advantage 95%). Freddie Mac remains one-unit only.
Fannie Mae SEL-2025-10 · Eligibility Matrix
Feb 4, 2026Freddie Mac lowers manufactured-home minimum to 400 sq ft
Freddie Mac cut the minimum above-grade finished area from 600 to 400 sq ft (Guide 5703.2) and announced the CHOICEHome alignment.
Freddie Mac Bulletin 2026-1 · Guide chapter 5703
Sep 15, 2025Updated HUD construction code takes effect
HUD’s update to the Manufactured Home Construction and Safety Standards (published Sept 16, 2024) took effect Sept 15, 2025, after a delay from Mar 17, 2025.
Final rule 2024-20545 · Delay 2025-03038
Sep 3, 2025Fannie Mae expands manufactured-home eligibility
MH Advantage is available for single-width homes, and CHOICEHome-labeled homes are accepted as equivalent to MH Advantage.
Fannie Mae SEL-2025-07 · Appraiser update (policy table)
May 2025USDA guarantees existing manufactured homes
USDA’s final rule (published Jan 3, 2025; Federal Register effective date Mar 4, 2025) was implemented in May 2025. It made the manufactured-housing pilot permanent and nationwide. Existing homes qualify if manufactured within 20 years of closing, at least 400 sq ft, never installed elsewhere, on a certified permanent foundation, unaltered except engineered or inspected porches and decks, and in the correct thermal (Uo) zone. They can be underwritten through GUS.
USDA bulletin (May 5, 2025) · USDA bulletin (Aug 21, 2025) · Final rule 2024-30270 · 7 CFR 3555.208
Mar 29, 2024FHA Title I loan limits raised and indexed
Title I Letter 488 raised the limits (home only: $105,532 single-section / $193,719 multi-section) and HUD now indexes them annually. Limits never go down. Current-year figures Verify.
