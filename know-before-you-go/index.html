Why now

Don't list first and find out later

Selling your home is the easy part. The surprise usually comes when it's time to buy the next one. Many sellers list, get an offer, and only then learn they can't get the new mortgage they assumed they could. Then the move is on a clock and the options are thin.

Credit issues
A late payment, a high card balance, or an old collection can change which loan programs you can use and what they cost. Some of it can be fixed, but it takes time you won't have once you're under contract.
Income changes
A new job, less overtime, retirement, or going self-employed changes how a lender counts your income. Better to hear that now than during underwriting.
Higher costs in the new area
Property taxes, insurance, HOA dues, and flood or wind coverage can be very different from where you live today, and they all count toward your qualifying payment.

What checking first gives you

  • A real price range for your next home, so you shop and list with confidence.
  • Time to fix things (pay down a card, correct a credit report error, document income) before they become deadlines.
  • Better timing choices, such as whether to sell first, buy first, or ask for a longer closing or a rent-back.
  • No rushed decisions and fewer deals falling apart late.

Checking with a loan officer does not commit you to anything. Your listing agent and I work together so your sale and your purchase fit.
