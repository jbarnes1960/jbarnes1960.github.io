Why now

Don't list first and find out later

Selling your home is the easy part. The surprise usually comes when it's time to buy the next one. Many sellers list, get an offer, and only then learn they can't get the new mortgage they assumed they could, or that their sale nets less than they planned. Then the move is on a clock and the options are thin. Here's what we check for, and why sooner is better:

Credit issues

A late payment, a high card balance, or an old collection can change which loan programs you can use and what they cost. Some of it can be fixed, but it takes time you won't have once you're under contract.

Income changes

A new job, less overtime, retirement, or going self-employed changes how a lender counts your income. Better to hear that now than during underwriting.

Higher costs in the new area

Property taxes, insurance, HOA dues, and flood or wind coverage can be very different from where you live today, and they all count toward your qualifying payment.

Unexpected liens

A judgment, an unpaid tax bill, past-due HOA dues, or a contractor's (mechanic's) lien can show up when the title company searches your home's records. Any of these can delay your closing or stop the sale until they're paid or cleared. Finding them early gives you time to sort them out.

Ask your title company for a preliminary title search before you list.
A higher payoff than you expect

What you owe on your mortgage, HELOC, or second loan is often more than the balance on your statement. Interest adds up daily, and there can be fees, an escrow shortage, or a prepayment charge. That means less cash from your sale for your next down payment.

Request a written payoff statement early, so your net proceeds and down payment are real numbers.
Problems you don't know about yet

A pre-listing home inspection finds issues before a buyer's inspector does, while you still have time to get repairs scheduled and done. See below.

Consider a pre-listing home inspection

An inspection before you go on the MLS shows you what a buyer's inspector will find, while you still control the timeline. Repairs, and especially insurance claims, take weeks to line up.

  • Hail or storm damage to the roof. It may be covered by insurance, but a claim means an adjuster visit, an estimate, and then a roofer's schedule.
  • Foundation movement or cracks. An engineer's report and repair plan can take time, and buyers and lenders will want to see them.
  • Anything that may need an insurance claim (water leaks, storm damage). Call your insurance company first, and give the claim time to work.
  • Fewer surprises later. Fixing items up front helps you avoid a last-minute price cut or repair request after the buyer's inspection.

What checking first gives you

  • A real price range for your next home, so you shop and list with confidence.
  • Time to fix things (pay down a card, correct a credit report error, clear a lien, schedule repairs) before they become deadlines.
  • Real numbers from your sale: an actual payoff and net proceeds, not a guess.
  • Better timing choices, such as whether to sell first, buy first, or ask for a longer closing or a rent-back.
  • No rushed decisions and fewer deals falling apart late.

Checking with a loan officer does not commit you to anything. Your listing agent and I work together so your sale and your purchase fit. A home inspection, title search, and payoff statement come from your inspector, title company, and lender; your listing agent can recommend people to call.
