Here's your November market snapshot for Colorado Springs & Pikes Peak.
Rates and market context
Colorado conventional pricing moved during Friday, Nov. 6, and ended at the same level as Thursday afternoon, according to my latest lender pricing review. That kind of movement is why I confirm each buyer's numbers in real time.
Mortgage News Daily reported on Nov 6 that the average lender's rates held at their lowest level since Sept 22 despite a much stronger-than-expected jobs report. Freddie Mac's weekly survey (Nov 5) showed a third straight weekly decline. Separately, MBS Highway's National Housing Index, a sentiment-based gauge of buyer activity and price direction, fell to 15 in October, its fifth straight monthly decline. It measures sentiment, not the number of buyers. These are recent readings, not forecasts.
Inventory: what the numbers show
These October 2026 figures for the Pikes Peak region (El Paso and Teller counties) are based on Pikes Peak REALTOR data, as summarized Nov 4 by a local brokerage:
- 4,110 active listings, up 5% from 3,918 a year earlier; about 5.0 months of supply
- 1,203 new listings, down 13%
- 816 closed sales, down 7%
- Median sale price of $455,000, down 4% from $473,500
- An average of 57 days to go under contract, compared with 54 a year earlier
- In September, 46.4% of active El Paso County listings had a price reduction, according to the Colorado Association of REALTORS
A local brokerage reviewing October Pikes Peak MLS activity estimated that roughly 7 of 10 transactions involved some form of seller concession (Colorado Real Estate Group). Treat that as a market observation rather than an official statistic.
What it means for your buyers and sellers
Buyers have more choice and sellers more competition. With price reductions and concessions common, well-prepared buyers can negotiate. Well-priced, well-presented homes are still selling, and clean, verifiable offers stand out.
Price cut or seller concession?
With the same financing assumptions, a lower price can reduce the loan amount and principal-and-interest payment. A seller concession is not cash handed to the buyer. Subject to program limits, it can be applied toward closing costs or a rate buydown, which may lower the payment. Which helps more depends on whether the buyer is short on cash or stretched on payment. Concession limits vary by loan program and down payment. I can run side-by-side scenarios first.
Look past the interest rate
Property taxes depend on the county, school district and any special or metro districts, common in newer communities. Many neighborhoods also carry HOA dues. Homeowners insurance can vary with roof type and age and wildfire exposure, so get a quote early in the inspection period. Ask for the tax amount, HOA dues and any metro district details before writing the offer.
Winning tight offers together
- Fully underwritten approval, when it fits: income, assets and credit reviewed by underwriting up front. Remaining conditions, such as appraisal, title, insurance and the final contract review, still apply, and approval is never a guarantee to close.
- Listing-agent call: I'll call the listing agent to learn what the seller values most: certainty, a closing date, a rent-back or fewer repair asks.
- Timelines I can verify: I'll confirm a realistic closing date before the offer goes in.
- Protections stay in: we compete on preparation and responsiveness, never by waiving inspection, appraisal or financing protections.
- Keep qualification steady: remind buyers to avoid new credit, big purchases, job changes or unexplained deposits until closing.
