Your Path to Closing

Pre-approval → condition & offer → inspection / appraisal → closing

1

Get pre-approved

Talk with James and complete the application so he can review income, assets and credit. A pre-approval shows which program fits, the real cash needed and the payment, including mortgage insurance and insurance quotes that are TBD in this kit.

2

See the home and confirm its condition

638 Woodmont is off market and has no photos, so condition is unknown. Renovated homes nearby closed at last list prices of $207,000–$255,000 and dated or as-is homes at $119,999–$178,999. Condition decides which group applies before any price is discussed.

3

Inspection and appraisal

If a contract is reached, a home inspection checks the roof, HVAC, plumbing, electrical and foundation. The lender's appraisal must support the price; FHA and VA appraisals also check that the home meets minimum safety and soundness standards, so a dated home may need repairs before closing.

4

Closing

You receive your Closing Disclosure at least three business days before closing. Do a final walk-through, bring your cash to close as instructed by the title company, sign the documents and get your keys.

Typical timeline
Timing depends on the contract dates, your documents, and appraisal and title turn times. Responding quickly to document requests is the best way to keep a closing date on track.

Pre-Approval Document Checklist

Having these ready speeds up approval. The exact list depends on the loan program and situation.

  • Government-issued photo ID (driver's license or passport) and Social Security number
  • Pay stubs for the most recent 30 days
  • W-2s for the last 2 years
  • Federal tax returns for the last 2 years (all pages and schedules)
  • Bank and savings statements for the last 2 months (all pages, even blank ones)
  • Statements for retirement and investment accounts (if used for down payment or reserves)
  • Gift letter and donor's bank statement, if any part of your funds is a gift
  • Self-employed or 1099 income: 2 years of returns, year-to-date profit-and-loss statement, and business bank statements if requested
  • Landlord name and contact, or 12 months of rent payments, and 2-year address history
  • VA buyers: Certificate of Eligibility (James can request it) and DD-214 or current statement of service
  • Divorce decree or child-support / alimony documents, if applicable
  • Letters of explanation for credit inquiries, gaps in employment or large deposits, if requested
At $220,000, the appraisal is the key risk

If 638 Woodmont is renovated, the Group A comps (last list $207,000–$255,000) suggest $220,000 could be supported. If it is dated, every Group B comp listed below $179,000, so an appraisal at $220,000 would be at risk. If an appraisal comes in low, the buyer can renegotiate, pay the difference in cash, or use the contract's financing protections. Ask James before writing an offer.
